# Lecturer guide

## Your courses

Open **Courses**. You see the deliveries you are assigned to for this term. A
course has two sides: the content learners read, and the assessments they sit.

## Building the course

**Edit content** opens the builder.

- A **section** is a week, a unit or a theme. Learners see nothing in it until
  you publish the section.
- A **lesson** sits inside a section. Publish it separately, so you can write
  next week's material while this week's is live.
- **Blocks** are what a lesson is made of: text, a callout, a file, an image,
  video, audio or a link. Add them in the order you want them read.

Publishing is the switch that matters. A section or lesson left as a draft is
invisible to learners, including to a learner who types its address directly.

Set a **release date** on a lesson that should open later, and an **available
from and until** window on a section that should open and close.

## Assessments

Add one from the course's assessment screen. Choose:

- **Out of** and **pass mark**: the marks themselves.
- **Weighting**: its share of the course mark. The plan is checked against 100%.
- **Due** and **closes**: the due date is when it is late; the closing date is
  when nothing more is accepted.
- **Late work**: accepted or not, and the penalty per day.
- **Attempts** and, for quizzes, a **time limit**.

It stays a draft until you publish it. Publishing also puts the due date on
learners' calendars.

### Quizzes

Questions live in **banks** and are reused. Add fixed questions, or a **pool**
that draws a different set for each learner. The paper is fixed when a learner
starts, so reloading cannot reroll it.

Eight question types mark themselves. Essays, long answers and file uploads come
to you as unmarked rather than being guessed at.

### Marking

**Assessments** in the sidebar is your queue, oldest first. Where a rubric is
attached it decides the mark, and you see the total it will produce before you
save.

Marks reach learners only when you **release results** for the whole
assessment. Until then a learner sees that their work arrived and nothing more,
which means you can mark a class over three days without anyone comparing
partial results.

## Attendance

Add a session, then take the register. Everyone enrolled is on it, unmarked.
Turn on **self check-in** and the platform gives you a code to read out; it
works from shortly before the session until part way through, so nobody marks
themselves present from home afterwards.

The report lists learners who have stopped coming before learners with a low
percentage. The one at 80% who has missed the last four sessions is the one to
phone.

## Discussions and messages

The course discussion is for questions the whole class benefits from. Private
messages are for the rest. Learners can reach you because you teach them.

Moderation is non-destructive: hiding a post leaves a visible gap, and a report
queues it for you rather than pulling it down.

## A few habits that help

- Publish the week's material before the week, not during it.
- Write the assessment weighting in at the start; the plan check will tell you
  when it does not add up to 100.
- Mark the whole class, then release. Releasing as you go invites comparison.
- If your marking drifted, say so in the moderation comments. It is a far easier
  conversation than a cohort adjustment later.
